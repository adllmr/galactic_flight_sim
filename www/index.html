Welcome to the Galactic Flight Simulator!

Navigate the RSS Lynden-Bell through the rotating barred potential.

Start at one of the five Lagrange points, where the ship can sit still in the frame rotating with the bar. L4 and L5 are stable orbits at the corotation resonance; L1 and L2 won't hold you for long. Which other orbits can you reach?

About

Gravitational potential

The barred potential implemented is taken from equation (5) in Williams & Evans (2017). This is produced by convolving a cored axisymmetric logarithmic potential with a needle density along the x-axis spanning [−a, a]. The bar length a is expressed in units of the core radius Rc. The asymptotic circular speed of the logarithmic potential is v0 = 1.

Rotation

By default the simulator is run in a frame corotating clockwise with the bar. This adds Coriolis and centrifugal forces to the equations of motion. The pattern speed is encoded in the ratio of the corotation radius to the bar length, R = RCR/a. Optionally the pattern speed can be accelerated or decelerated with time. In this case the spaceship also experiences an Euler force. The deceleration rate of the bar is quantified by the dimensionless deceleration parameter η = ṘCR/v0.

Lagrange points

In the frame rotating with the bar, a ship at rest feels the bar's gravity and the centrifugal force. The Lagrange points are where these cancel: the stationary points of the effective potential Φeff (the yellow contours).

L1 and L2 lie on the bar's long axis, a little beyond the corotation radius. They are saddle points of Φeff, so a ship left there drifts away.

L3 is the centre, the lowest point of Φeff. A nudged ship just oscillates about it.

L4 and L5 lie on the bar's short axis at exactly the corotation radius RCR. They are peaks of Φeff, yet stable: the Coriolis force turns a falling ship sideways into a banana-shaped orbit around the point.

The simulator finds L1 and L2 numerically and judges each point's stability from the curvature of Φeff and the pattern speed Ω, so both update when you change the bar. The labels follow the usual convention for barred galaxies.

The elliptical galaxy

The elliptical uses a flattened logarithmic potential, Φ = ½ v02 ln(Rc2 + x2 + y2/q2) (Binney & Tremaine, Galactic Dynamics). It has a flat core and a flat rotation curve, like a galaxy embedded in a dark-matter halo. The axis ratio q of the potential can go down to 1/√2 ≈ 0.71; any flatter and the density it implies would be negative somewhere. The stars are roughly three times as flattened as the potential.

The galaxy does not rotate, so there are no Coriolis or centrifugal forces and no Lagrange points. Instead you start on one of its orbit families: loop orbits circulate the centre, box orbits have no angular momentum and plunge through the centre, and orbits along the long and short axes oscillate through it. The orbit along the short axis is unstable.

An optional central black hole, softened over 3 units, can be added. It scatters orbits that pass close to the centre, so box and axis orbits can turn chaotic.

Each start is labelled stable or unstable by following it alongside a copy displaced by 0.001 units: regular orbits stay within about 0.1 units of each other, unstable ones separate by hundreds.

The background

The cyan line indicates the future orbit of the spaceship, if no thrust is applied.

The yellow points indicate contours of the effective potential, including a centrifugal term.

The animated stars (off by default) follow circular paths at approximately the circular speed of the background logarithmic potential.

The radius at which the stars are stationary in the rotating frame indicates the corotation radius.
